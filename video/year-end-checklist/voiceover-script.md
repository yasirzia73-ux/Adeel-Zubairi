## Script (one line per caption)

### Hook
- December 31 is a money deadline most people ignore.
- Miss it, and some of your money can simply disappear.
- So here are 5 money moves to make before the year ends.
- The first one could be free money already sitting in your account.

### Intro
- Maya's back. This time, she's doing a year-end money check.
- Five boxes to tick. One deadline. Let's go.

### Move 1: FSA
- Move one: spend your FSA money.
- A flexible spending account lets you pay for health costs with pre-tax dollars.
- But it's use it or lose it.
- Some plans let you carry up to $680 into next year. Others give you a short grace period. Some give you neither.
- Whatever is left beyond that is simply gone.
- So check your balance now. Glasses, contacts, prescriptions, a dentist visit, even sunscreen can qualify.

### Move 2: 401(k)
- Move two: boost your 401(k) before your last paycheck.
- In 2026, you can put up to $24,500 of your own pay into a 401(k).
- It comes straight out of your paycheck, so your last December paycheck is the real deadline.
- In the 22% tax bracket, every extra $1,000 into a traditional 401(k) cuts this year's tax bill by about $220.
- And at the very least, make sure you got your full employer match. That part is free money.

### Move 3: Tax-loss harvesting
- Move three: harvest your losses.
- Got an investment in a regular brokerage account that lost value?
- Selling it before December 31 locks in a loss you can actually use.
- Losses cancel out gains. And up to $3,000 of extra loss can lower your regular taxable income.
- Just watch the wash-sale rule: buy the same investment back within 30 days, and the loss doesn't count.
- This only works in taxable accounts, not inside your 401(k) or IRA.

### Move 4: Give
- Move four: give before the year ends.
- Donations only count for 2026 if you make them by December 31.
- And here's what's new: starting in 2026, even if you don't itemize, you can deduct up to $1,000 in cash gifts to charity.
- Married and filing jointly? Up to $2,000.
- Keep the receipt, and make sure the charity qualifies.

### Move 5: Next year
- Move five: set up next year before it starts.
- Open enrollment is usually your one chance a year to change your benefits.
- If you pick a high-deductible health plan, look at an HSA.
- Unlike an FSA, HSA money never expires. It rolls over year after year.
- Then raise your 401(k) contribution by just 1% for January. You'll barely notice it.

### Bonus
- Bonus: one deadline that is NOT December 31.
- You can still put money into an IRA for 2026 until April 15, 2027.
- Up to $7,500. So that one can wait for tax season.

### Recap and end
- Let's recap.
- Use your FSA. Boost your 401(k). Harvest losses. Give to charity. Set up next year.
- Tax rules change and every situation is different, so double-check on IRS.gov or with a tax pro.
- Tick off even one of these before December 31, and you start next year ahead.
- Subscribe for the next one: 401(k) explained, and the free money most people miss.
